# Zanistarast.com — Cultural and Moral Visual Policy

Status: LOCKED — Roadmap item 15

## Principle

Every visual representation published on zanistarast.com must preserve human dignity, cultural truth and the project's governing moral boundary. Visual quality is not measured only by realism or cinematic appeal; framing, costume, gesture, context and narrative function also matter.

Within this project's declared framework, visual publication must remain compatible with the applicable Qur'anic rulings, Prophetic ethics, and the standards of edep, haya and terbiye.

Mira may implement and check this policy, but it must not invent a religious ruling where the governing rule or its application is uncertain. Such cases remain pending for Rasterast and, where required, Müdebbir review.

## Scope

This policy applies to:

- films, short films and documentaries;
- animation and cartoons;
- AI-generated or AI-assisted images/video;
- illustrations and photography;
- thumbnails, posters and promotional stills;
- storyboards and concept art intended for production;
- avatars/character imagery;
- interactive visual media;
- clips, trailers and derivatives.

A short promotional asset is not exempt from the policy.

## Human dignity

Women, men and children must be represented as persons rather than visual commodities. Production must avoid degrading, humiliating, voyeuristic or sexually objectifying framing.

The camera, crop, pose, animation and editing must not deliberately emphasize intimate/body areas for attraction or engagement.

## Clothing and appearance

Costume decisions must jointly consider:

1. the project's applicable modesty/edep/haya standard;
2. the represented culture;
3. historical period;
4. geography and climate;
5. age and role;
6. narrative necessity.

Contemporary fashion is not the automatic default. For culturally situated works, appropriate local/traditional clothing should be researched under Items 13–14.

Historical/cultural authenticity does not automatically authorize a depiction that conflicts with the platform's governing publication boundary. Difficult conflicts must be reviewed rather than silently distorted or automatically admitted.

## Women and men

The policy applies consistently to women and men. Neither may be sexualized, reduced to physical display or used as decorative attraction.

Character strength, beauty, dignity, work, family role, courage, vulnerability and moral conflict may be portrayed without turning the body into the primary object of attention.

## Children

Children require heightened protection. Visual production must never sexualize a child or use adult-coded seductive posing, framing or costume treatment.

Children may be shown playing, learning, working within appropriate historical/cultural contexts, facing challenges and expressing emotion, while preserving dignity and age-appropriate presentation.

## Camera and composition

Review must consider not only what a character wears, but how the image is constructed:

- camera angle and distance;
- crop and focal emphasis;
- pose and gesture;
- lighting where it changes the meaning of the depiction;
- slow motion/repetition;
- thumbnail selection;
- editing sequence;
- surrounding dialogue/audio.

A nominally acceptable costume cannot make an objectifying shot acceptable.

## Wrongdoing and difficult realities

A story may depict bad character, injustice, temptation, violence, social pressure or other wrongdoing when needed for truthful narrative and moral understanding.

Depiction is not endorsement. Production should make the narrative function and consequences intelligible without glamorizing wrongdoing, eroticizing it, or using it as engagement bait.

Necessary depiction should use the least sensational level that still preserves truth and narrative clarity.

## Violence and suffering

Violence, injury, death, war, persecution and suffering require proportionality. Graphic detail must not be added merely for shock, spectacle or retention.

Audience suitability rules from Item 12 remain applicable. Documentary truth may require difficult material, but it still requires contextual and audience review.

## Sacred and religious contexts

Religious scenes require heightened source and representation review. Mira must not visually invent a sacred event, religious practice, ruling or attributed act merely because it produces a compelling scene.

This policy does not itself authorize depiction of Prophets or other sensitive sacred figures/events. Such representation requires an explicit applicable rule/review before production.

## Cultural integrity

Moral review and cultural review operate together. Another people must not be mocked, exoticized or visually assimilated for convenience. Clothing, architecture, landscape and material culture remain governed by Item 13.

For eligible anonymous/designated works using the Kurdistan default, Item 14 applies without rewriting source provenance.

## Generation constraints for Mira

Before final visual generation, Mira should receive structured constraints covering:

- work and scene identity;
- culture/place/period;
- character role and age context;
- approved costume direction;
- prohibited visual treatments;
- moral/narrative purpose;
- source/provenance constraints;
- audience context;
- rights/reference restrictions.

Generation prompts are production instructions, not the policy authority. Output must be reviewed after generation because a compliant prompt does not guarantee a compliant image/video.

## Review outcomes

A visual review may return:

- ACCEPTED;
- NEEDS_REVISION;
- ESCALATED;
- REJECTED;
- WITHDRAWN after publication if a material issue is discovered.

Review should retain the representation/version reviewed and concise reasons. Revised assets require review of the new version.

## Recommendation and thumbnails

Recommendation systems cannot use a more provocative thumbnail or crop than the underlying work permits. Engagement performance cannot waive visual-policy requirements.

Automatically selected thumbnails must themselves pass the applicable visual checks before public use.

## Item 15 acceptance criteria

1. The policy governs all visual media and derivatives.
2. Qur'anic-ruling, Prophetic-ethics, edep, haya and terbiye boundaries remain binding.
3. Mira cannot invent religious rulings to resolve uncertainty.
4. Women, men and children are protected from objectifying presentation.
5. Children receive heightened protection from sexualized/adult-coded depiction.
6. Clothing review includes culture, period, geography, age and moral boundary.
7. Camera/framing/editing are reviewed independently of costume.
8. Wrongdoing can be depicted without being glamorized.
9. Violence/suffering require purpose and proportionality.
10. Sacred representation requires explicit applicable review and is not authorized by this document alone.
11. Mira generation receives structured constraints and its output is reviewed afterward.
12. Thumbnails/promotional assets cannot bypass the policy.
13. Revised visual assets require version-specific re-review.
14. Cultural authenticity and moral review operate together.
