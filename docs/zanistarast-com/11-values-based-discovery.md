# Zanistarast.com — Values-Based Discovery and Categories

Status: LOCKED — Roadmap item 11

## Principle

People should be able to discover meaningful content using ordinary human language rather than knowing the internal Zanistarast scientific vocabulary. Discovery labels organize navigation; they do not redefine ontology, truth status or religious authority.

## Primary discovery dimensions

The catalogue should support overlapping discovery dimensions rather than forcing every work into one folder.

### Values and ethical questions
Examples include:
- mercy and compassion;
- justice and fairness;
- truthfulness;
- trust and keeping one's word;
- modesty, manners and haya;
- discipline and upbringing;
- patience and self-restraint;
- courage;
- responsibility;
- generosity and solidarity;
- forgiveness;
- family;
- friendship and loyalty;
- respect for parents, children, elders and neighbours;
- care for society and creation.

This vocabulary is extensible and must be reviewed before public use.

### Format
Film, short film, documentary, animation, cartoon, story, audio narration, readable article, illustration, infographic, interactive work and future admitted forms.

### Audience/context
Children, young people, families, adults and other carefully defined contexts may be supported without implying that every work is suitable for every audience.

### Cultural/source context
Visitors may discover works through their people/culture, geography, historical period, language, author/source tradition or collection where this metadata is known and appropriate to expose.

### Collections
Editorial collections may include concepts such as World Stories, Kurdish Stories, Children's Stories, World Classics, Films, Animations, Documentaries or thematic series. Names are public navigation aids, not epistemic classifications.

## Multi-tag model

A work may belong to several values and discovery dimensions. A story can concern honesty, friendship and responsibility at once. The system must not force a single “correct category” when the work genuinely spans several themes.

Theme assignments should be reviewable and traceable to editorial/Mira reasoning rather than generated solely from engagement optimization.

## Home discovery

The future home page may assemble horizontal/vertical discovery groups such as:
- Continue watching;
- Because you liked a theme/work;
- Mercy stories;
- Family viewing;
- Short works;
- World stories;
- New or newly approved works;
- Films/animations/documentaries;
- editorially curated ethical themes.

Personalized rows may only select from content already admitted for that user/context.

## Search and filters

Future search should support title, plain-language topic, value/theme, source/author, culture/people, format, language and appropriate audience context.

Filters should be combinable. Search ranking may use relevance and preference signals but must not reinterpret publication/Rasterast status.

## Religious and ethical boundary

Discovery taxonomy and recommendation labels remain subject to the platform's governing framework. No category, promotional label or recommendation packaging may be used to normalize content that fails the applicable Qur'anic-ruling and Prophetic-ethics publication constraints.

Mira must not create a religious ruling merely by assigning a theme label. Religious/legal interpretation remains within the applicable Rasterast/Müdebbir review process.

## Cultural respect

Cultures must not be reduced to stereotypes for easier browsing. A cultural label should reflect documented provenance/context. Unknown or mixed provenance may be represented honestly rather than guessed.

## Avoid manipulative categorization

The platform should not create sensational categories solely to maximize clicks, outrage or compulsive consumption. Discovery should help people find useful works and understand why they are related.

## Personalization boundary

Likes and viewing choices may increase the visibility of similar values, formats or works for that visitor. Personalization cannot:
- admit unapproved content;
- alter canonical theme/source metadata;
- hide required provenance;
- treat popularity as moral/scientific authority;
- override age/context restrictions.

The user should retain practical ways to move outside personalized suggestions through search, categories and editorial discovery.

## Item 11 acceptance criteria

1. Ordinary values provide first-class public discovery.
2. Discovery labels do not replace Zanistarast ontology.
3. A work can carry multiple themes/categories.
4. Format, audience, culture/source and collections are independent dimensions.
5. Search/filter architecture can combine those dimensions.
6. Home-page rows can support editorial and later personalized discovery.
7. Personalization operates only within admitted content.
8. Cultural provenance is not guessed or stereotyped.
9. Religious/ethical publication gates cannot be bypassed by taxonomy or promotion.
10. Visitors can discover beyond their personalized feed.
