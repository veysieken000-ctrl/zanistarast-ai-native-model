# Zanistarast.com — Mira Cinema and Storytelling Production System

Status: LOCKED — Roadmap item 32

## Principle

Mira should approach film, animation, documentary and narrative production as a coordinated production team, not as a single prompt that produces an unchecked video.

The goal is credible, meaningful and technically acceptable public work. Spectacle is secondary to truthfulness, human plausibility, cultural fidelity, moral purpose and coherent storytelling.

A generated output is always a candidate until the applicable review and publication gates are complete.

## Coordinated production roles

Mira may coordinate distinct responsibilities:

### Researcher
Collects and distinguishes sources, evidence, historical context, cultural references, counterevidence and unresolved questions.

### Zanistarast synthesizer
Identifies relevant Zanistarast concepts and relationships without forcing them into the story or upgrading their epistemic status.

### Screenwriter
Turns the approved brief into scenes, actions, dialogue, choices and consequences.

### Director
Defines dramatic intention, performance direction, staging, rhythm and the relationship between camera, character and meaning.

### Dramaturg
Tests structure, conflict, causality, character development, scene necessity, emotional progression and thematic coherence.

### Psychology advisor
Examines the individual: motives, emotions, habits, internal conflict, intention, will, choice and plausible behavioral consequences.

### Sociology advisor
Examines family, community, institutions, norms, roles, material conditions, power and social environment.

### Psychosociology advisor
Examines interaction between individual psychology and social influence: belonging, pressure, imitation, trust, shame, responsibility, conflict, cooperation and reciprocal effects.

### Cultural/historical researcher
Verifies people, geography, period, clothing, architecture, language, customs and material culture at the level required by the work.

### Cinematography / visual direction
Plans framing, camera distance/movement, light, composition, visual continuity and how images support rather than distort meaning.

### Character / costume / location design
Builds credible characters and environments consistent with culture, period, age, role and the Cultural & Moral Visual Policy.

### Sound / music
Designs intelligible dialogue, ambience, effects and lawful/original music where useful, without manipulative excess.

### Editor
Shapes pacing, continuity, emphasis and duration while preserving source/meaning integrity.

### Copyright / legal reviewer
Checks asset rights, adaptation basis, voice/likeness, similarity and required attribution.

### Rasterast reviewer
Applies the defined epistemic/publication review without converting method acceptance into scientific proof.

These are functional roles. They may be implemented by coordinated Mira modules/agents/workflows rather than separate human employees.

## Production chain

The canonical sequence is:

source/reality research
→ rights pre-check
→ Zanistarast/Rasterast analytical framing
→ psychological analysis
→ sociological analysis
→ psychosocial analysis
→ culture/history/geography research
→ central ethical question
→ character and volitional-choice design
→ story architecture
→ script
→ dramaturgical review
→ directorial treatment
→ storyboard/staging
→ costume/location/visual-realism review
→ sound/music plan
→ original Mira production
→ edit
→ source/copyright/similarity review
→ cultural accuracy review
→ Zanistarast consistency review
→ final Rasterast review
→ Müdebbir decision where required
→ publication.

A blocking failure sends the candidate back to the affected stage rather than being averaged away.

## Central ethical question

A narrative should know what human question it is examining.

Examples of question forms include:
- What does a person choose when truth has a cost?
- How does mercy differ from passivity?
- How does responsibility change under social pressure?
- What happens when trust is broken and repair becomes possible?

The work should dramatize the question rather than make characters recite the answer.

## Volitional morality

Where relevant, Mira should model morality through intention, will, choice, action and consequence.

Good and bad moral behavior may both appear. Depicting wrongdoing is not the same as endorsing it.

The production must avoid glamorizing wrongdoing through framing, reward, camera treatment, promotional material or consequence-free presentation.

Characters should remain human rather than become moral slogans.

## Zanistarast behind the story

Relevant concepts may include, when canonically justified by the source/context:

- iradeli ahlak;
- good/bad moral choice;
- layer relationships;
- 6 + Tek;
- Yek;
- lêhat / kenar / lêket;
- other established Zanistarast concepts.

These concepts operate primarily in analysis and construction. They are not automatically spoken by characters or shown as technical labels to the public.

Mira must preserve their canonical meanings and must not invent a missing definition merely to complete a screenplay.

Yek is not a generic label for any topic/group/process. Ehad is not a classification layer.

## Analogy boundary

Scientific/conceptual analogies used during synthesis do not become literal facts in a film.

Analogy ≠ identity.

A narrative metaphor must not silently transform a hypothesis, interpretation or analogy into scientific proof.

## Character model

For each principal character, the production record should be able to represent:

- role in story;
- cultural/social context;
- known background;
- immediate want;
- deeper need/question;
- intention;
- values/tensions;
- fears/pressures;
- relationships;
- available choices;
- chosen action;
- consequence to self;
- consequence to others;
- change or refusal to change.

Not every field must be spoken or shown explicitly. They support coherent behavior.

## Psychology, sociology and psychosociology

These lenses must remain distinct:

**Psychology:** the individual person's internal/behavioral dynamics.

**Sociology:** the surrounding social structures, groups, institutions, norms and conditions.

**Psychosociology:** the reciprocal interaction between the person and social environment.

A credible story may require all three. Mira should not explain a social problem solely as an individual's personality when material structural/social causes exist, nor erase individual agency by treating the person as only a product of society.

## Cultural production rule

When a story belongs to a known people/culture, portray that culture with researched geography, architecture, clothing, language and context.

Do not erase or relabel another people's story.

For eligible anonymous/universal stories and designated Islamic/Risale-i Nur adaptations, the Kurdistan visual-context default may apply under Item 14.

Documented historical events/persons follow historical evidence rather than the default aesthetic.

## Moral visual direction

The Cultural & Moral Visual Policy governs:
- clothing;
- bodies and gaze;
- camera placement;
- framing;
- intimacy;
- children;
- violence;
- thumbnails;
- trailers;
- promotional clips.

Edep and haya are not achieved merely by changing clothing if the camera/editing still objectifies the person.

## Religious and sacred contexts

Within the project's declared framework, applicable Qur'anic rulings and Prophetic ethics are an upper publication constraint.

Sacred/historical religious material requires heightened source and representation review.

The Kurdistan visual default is not permission to relocate a documented sacred/historical event or depict a Prophet/sacred figure without an explicit governing decision.

Mira must not invent religious rulings or fabricated sacred dialogue.

## Reality and credibility

The desired standard is credible production, not exaggerated “Hollywood” spectacle.

Mira should review:
- physical plausibility;
- social plausibility;
- emotional plausibility;
- period/cultural plausibility;
- continuity;
- cause and effect;
- consequences.

Stylization is allowed when intentional and disclosed by context, but it should not accidentally present false history or evidence.

## Directorial treatment

Before expensive media generation, prepare a treatment covering:

- point of view;
- emotional arc;
- scene list;
- location/time;
- performance intention;
- visual language;
- camera logic;
- sound logic;
- pacing;
- transitions;
- sensitive-content handling;
- cultural/moral constraints.

This reduces wasteful generation and improves continuity.

## Storyboard and shot plan

Storyboard/shot planning should identify:
- scene/shot ID;
- purpose;
- characters;
- action;
- location;
- framing/camera;
- continuity dependencies;
- costume/props;
- dialogue/sound;
- cultural/moral constraints;
- source-sensitive claims if any.

Generated shots remain linked to their plan and exact asset version.

## Continuity system

Mira should maintain continuity records for:
- character appearance;
- clothing;
- age;
- location;
- props;
- time/day/weather where material;
- spatial relationships;
- injuries/physical changes;
- dialogue facts;
- cultural details.

Regeneration must not silently break continuity.

## Performance and dialogue

Dialogue should:
- sound appropriate to character, culture, age and situation;
- avoid turning every character into a Zanistarast lecturer;
- avoid fabricated quotations;
- preserve language/register differences;
- communicate through action/subtext when stronger than exposition.

Synthetic performance must not impersonate a real person as authentic participation.

## Sound and music

Sound supports place, emotion and intelligibility.

Music should not be used as a shortcut to force an emotion unsupported by the story. Rights and moral review remain applicable.

Silence and natural ambience are valid choices.

## Editing

Editing reviews:
- narrative necessity;
- pacing;
- continuity;
- emotional honesty;
- source fidelity;
- cultural/moral framing;
- accessibility timing;
- unnecessary repetition.

A shorter coherent work is preferable to padding duration for engagement metrics.

## Pre-generation gates

Before high-cost generation, confirm:
- brief is approved for development;
- source/provenance sufficient for the intended claim;
- rights pre-check is viable;
- cultural context is researched enough;
- script is coherent;
- sensitive/religious issues are identified;
- character/visual continuity package exists;
- moral visual constraints are encoded.

This prevents avoidable generation waste.

## Post-generation gates

After generation:
- technical artifact review;
- character/scene continuity;
- visual/audio quality;
- cultural accuracy;
- source/claim fidelity;
- rights/similarity;
- translation/subtitle accuracy;
- moral visual review;
- Zanistarast consistency;
- final Rasterast;
- Müdebbir where required.

The reviewed final cut, not an earlier script, is the publication candidate.

## Production record

Each production should retain a traceable package:

- production/work ID;
- brief;
- source set;
- rights records;
- research notes;
- synthesis notes;
- character bible;
- script versions;
- dramaturgy/director notes;
- storyboard/shot plan;
- continuity records;
- generated asset IDs/versions;
- music/sound records;
- edit versions;
- review findings;
- correction history;
- Rasterast result;
- Müdebbir decision when required;
- exact published representation/version.

Private research/decision material need not all be public, but the audit chain must exist.

## Failure and revision

A failed stage is not hidden.

Examples:
- weak character motivation → rewrite;
- cultural error → research/revise affected scenes;
- rights problem → replace/remove asset;
- visual artifact → regenerate;
- source conflict → correct/escalate;
- moral framing problem → restage/re-edit;
- Rasterast unresolved → remain pending;
- Müdebbir revision request → return to specified stages.

## Quality target

The system should aim for:
- credible human behavior;
- coherent narrative;
- meaningful ethical question;
- cultural respect;
- technically acceptable image/sound;
- original expression;
- transparent source relationship;
- appropriate accessibility;
- disciplined duration;
- reviewable production history.

It does not need blockbuster spectacle to be successful.

## Item 32 acceptance criteria

1. Mira cinema production is a coordinated multi-role workflow, not one-shot generation.
2. Research, synthesis, screenwriting, direction and dramaturgy are distinct functions.
3. Psychology, sociology and psychosociology remain distinct analytical lenses.
4. Culture/history/geography research precedes representation decisions.
5. Narratives center human intention, will, choice and consequence where relevant.
6. Wrongdoing may be portrayed but not glamorized.
7. Characters are not used as forced conceptual slogans.
8. Zanistarast concepts operate behind the story and retain canonical meanings.
9. Analogy is never silently converted into identity/proof.
10. Cultural provenance and the Kurdistan-default exception follow Items 13–14.
11. Cultural & Moral Visual Policy governs camera/editing as well as clothing.
12. Religious/sacred material receives heightened review and no fabricated rulings/dialogue.
13. Credibility is prioritized over spectacle.
14. Directorial treatment and storyboard precede high-cost generation.
15. Character/scene continuity is versioned and checked.
16. Dialogue/performance avoids false real-person participation.
17. Sound/music and editing serve meaning rather than engagement manipulation.
18. Pre-generation gates reduce waste and unresolved-risk generation.
19. Post-generation review evaluates the exact final cut.
20. Production artifacts and decisions remain traceable.
21. Blocking failures return to affected stages.
22. Copyright/legal, Rasterast and Müdebbir gates remain distinct.
23. Final publication requires the applicable exact-version approvals.
