# Zanistarast.com — Audience and Age-Appropriate Presentation

Status: LOCKED — Roadmap item 12

## Principle

The same underlying work may require different presentation for a child, young person, family or adult. Adaptation may change vocabulary, pacing, examples, duration, visual intensity and explanatory depth; it must not silently change source meaning, evidence status, moral boundary or provenance.

Age/context labels are guidance and publication controls, not judgments about a person's worth, intelligence or moral standing.

## Audience profiles

The architecture may support reviewed audience contexts such as:

- children;
- young people;
- family/co-viewing;
- adults;
- general audience;
- specialist/deeper reading where appropriate.

Exact age bands, if introduced, must be deliberately defined and reviewable rather than guessed by Mira from appearance or personal data.

## Adaptation dimensions

An audience-specific representation may vary:

- vocabulary and sentence complexity;
- narrative length and pacing;
- amount of background explanation;
- examples and metaphors;
- visual intensity;
- depiction versus off-screen treatment of difficult events;
- audio/music intensity;
- interaction complexity;
- reading length;
- amount and placement of deeper source detail.

These changes create a versioned representation/variant linked to the same canonical work or, if meaning changes materially, a separately reviewed adaptation.

## Invariants

Audience adaptation must preserve:

- the central meaning of the approved work;
- source/provenance relationships;
- distinction between fact, interpretation, hypothesis, analogy and fiction;
- meaningful uncertainty and caveats;
- cultural identity and historical truth where applicable;
- copyright/rights requirements;
- Rasterast and publication constraints;
- the applicable Qur'anic-ruling and Prophetic-ethics boundary of this project.

“Made for children” is never permission to fabricate a religious ruling, scientific certainty or historical fact.

## Children

Children's content should favor clarity, safety, constructive moral understanding and developmentally suitable presentation. It should not use fear, humiliation, graphic imagery, manipulative guilt or addictive interaction patterns as teaching shortcuts.

Wrongdoing may be represented when necessary to understand choice and consequence, but it should not be glamorized. Moral understanding should emerge naturally through comprehensible actions and consequences rather than constant slogan-like preaching.

## Young people

Youth-oriented work may address greater complexity, internal conflict, social pressure, responsibility, intention and consequences. It should respect the audience's capacity to think rather than using either childish simplification or sensationalism.

## Family/co-viewing

Family presentation should be suitable for shared viewing within its declared context. It may include prompts or explanations that help adults and children discuss a work together without replacing the work with a lecture.

## Adults

Adult presentation may include more complex moral, social, historical and psychological material. “Adult” must not become a route around the platform's moral/visual publication standards.

## Sensitive subjects

War, death, injustice, abuse, grief, illness, persecution or other difficult realities may sometimes be essential to a truthful work. Their inclusion requires purpose, proportionality and audience-specific review.

The platform should distinguish educational/narrative necessity from shock value. Graphic detail should not be added merely to increase engagement.

## Cultural and visual standards

Audience adaptation must not erase a story's people, geography, clothing, architecture or historical period. The later Cultural & Moral Visual Policy applies across all audience levels.

Within this project's framework, edep/haya/terbiye requirements remain applicable to costume, framing, dialogue and behavior presentation; age adaptation cannot bypass them.

## Metadata and review

Each audience-specific representation/variant should be able to record:

- intended audience/context;
- suitability rationale;
- sensitive-topic descriptors where useful;
- adaptation/version relationship;
- reviewer/status;
- accessibility needs;
- language;
- cultural context;
- applicable content warnings presented without sensationalism.

Mira may propose an audience classification, but uncertain or consequential classifications must remain reviewable and may be escalated.

## Personalization and privacy

Recommendation may use an explicitly available audience/profile context to filter already admitted works. The system must not infer a child's age from face, voice or other biometric appearance for content classification.

Audience filtering cannot override canonical publication or rights status.

## Item 12 acceptance criteria

1. Audience-specific presentation is separate from canonical work identity.
2. Vocabulary, pacing, examples and intensity may vary without changing meaning/status.
3. Children, youth, family, adult and general contexts are representable.
4. Children's content excludes fear/humiliation/manipulative guilt as teaching shortcuts.
5. Wrongdoing may be portrayed but not glamorized.
6. Sensitive subjects require purpose, proportionality and audience review.
7. Adult classification cannot bypass moral/visual standards.
8. Cultural/historical identity remains intact across age adaptations.
9. Qur'anic-ruling and Prophetic-ethics constraints remain applicable across all audiences.
10. Mira may propose but does not fabricate certainty in audience classification.
11. Age is not inferred from biometric appearance for this purpose.
12. Audience metadata remains versioned, reviewable and subordinate to publication gates.
