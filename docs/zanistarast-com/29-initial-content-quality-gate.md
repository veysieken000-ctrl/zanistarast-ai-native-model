# Zanistarast.com — Initial Content Quality Gate

Status: LOCKED — Roadmap item 29

## Principle

zanistarast.com must not create the appearance of abundance by publishing weak, repetitive or insufficiently reviewed AI content.

The first public works establish the platform's quality norm. A small number of credible, useful and carefully reviewed works is preferable to a large catalogue of low-value outputs.

Publication count is not a success metric by itself.

## Pilot-first rule

Before any high-volume production, .com should operate a deliberately small pilot set covering different representation types, for example:

- one short narrative/film candidate;
- one animation or illustrated story candidate;
- one audio/narrated candidate;
- one public reading/explanation candidate.

These are format-validation targets, not mandatory quotas. A candidate exists only when a suitable source, purpose and distinct contribution justify it.

## Candidate brief

Every initial work candidate must begin with a brief that records:

- intended public value/question;
- target audience;
- proposed medium and why that medium helps;
- source/provenance class;
- cultural/historical setting;
- language plan;
- intended emotional register;
- expected public learning/understanding outcome;
- rights basis;
- known uncertainty/risk;
- what makes this work worth producing rather than duplicating an existing work.

If the last question has no convincing answer, do not produce another near-duplicate.

## Quality dimensions

Initial works are reviewed across multiple dimensions rather than a single opaque score:

### Meaning and purpose
The work has a clear ethical/public purpose and does not reduce a value to a slogan.

### Source fidelity
Source, adaptation, commentary and invention are distinguishable. Claims do not exceed their evidence.

### Narrative quality
Where narrative is used, characters have intelligible motives, choices and consequences. Dialogue should sound human rather than function as a lecture.

### Psychological and social credibility
Behavior, conflict and consequences should be plausible for the represented people/context. Psychology, sociology and psychosocial interaction are considered where material.

### Cultural and historical credibility
Clothing, place, architecture, customs, language and period are researched at the level required by the work. Unknown details are not presented as established fact.

### Visual quality
Composition, continuity, anatomy/object consistency, costume, camera/framing and scene coherence meet the intended medium's needs. AI artifacts that materially damage credibility require revision.

### Audio quality
Speech is intelligible and appropriate; music/effects do not manipulate, overwhelm or falsely imitate protected recordings/performers.

### Editing and pacing
The work respects the audience's time. Repetition, filler and unnecessary duration are removed.

### Public-language clarity
The audience can understand the work without needing specialist Zanistarast vocabulary. Technical concepts are translated faithfully rather than erased or exaggerated.

### Accessibility
Applicable captions, transcripts, readable text, keyboard semantics and alternative access are prepared.

### Originality and rights
The work does not closely reproduce protected expression. References are research inputs, not copying permission.

### Moral and religious compatibility
Within the project's governing framework, the complete representation and its derivatives comply with the Cultural & Moral Visual Policy and applicable Qur'anic-ruling/Prophetic-ethics review requirements.

### Epistemic integrity
Scientific, historical, religious-source and interpretive claims preserve their actual status. Uncertainty is not converted into certainty for dramatic effect.

## No aggregate score shortcut

A high result in visual polish cannot compensate for a rights violation, source fabrication, serious cultural error or failed moral/Rasterast gate.

Quality dimensions may use structured findings, but blocking gates remain blocking.

## AI-specific review

Mira/AI output receives explicit review for:

- fabricated citations/quotations;
- invented historical or cultural detail;
- character/scene continuity errors;
- visual artifacts;
- accidental imitation of known protected expression;
- synthetic voice identity concerns;
- mistranslation;
- false confidence;
- repetitive/template-like writing;
- emotional manipulation;
- mismatch between source and generated scene.

AI generation is a production technique, not evidence of quality.

## Originality / repetition check

Before approval, compare a candidate with existing .com works and production candidates.

Review:
- central ethical question;
- plot/argument structure;
- key scenes/examples;
- visual concept;
- title/thumbnail;
- narration/dialogue;
- source set;
- intended learning outcome.

If a candidate merely restates an existing work without a meaningful new public contribution, merge, revise or stop it.

## End-to-end pilot review

The first pilot works should traverse the complete applicable chain:

brief → research → rights pre-check → culture/history → Zanistarast synthesis where applicable → script/adaptation → moral visual constraints → production → edit → source/similarity review → cultural review → accessibility → Rasterast → Müdebbir where required → exact-version publication decision.

A pilot is useful even when it is rejected: the rejection should improve the process rather than be hidden.

## Exact-version review

Review approval attaches to the exact candidate version. Regenerating scenes, changing narration, replacing music, altering translation or materially editing text can reopen affected reviews.

“AI made only a small change” is not a governance exemption.

## Thumbnails and promotion

The quality gate also covers:
- thumbnails;
- trailers;
- short clips;
- captions/titles/descriptions;
- promotional stills.

A restrained main work must not be marketed through misleading, sensational or morally incompatible derivatives.

## Initial catalogue rule

Do not bulk-generate hundreds of placeholder-looking public works.

Development fixtures remain visibly DEMO and are never counted as public catalogue content.

The initial public catalogue grows only from works that individually pass the applicable gates.

## Feedback after publication

Early audience feedback may identify:
- confusing explanations;
- accessibility failures;
- technical playback problems;
- cultural errors;
- factual/source concerns.

Feedback is a review signal, not a vote on truth or morality. Likes/watch time do not lower the quality threshold.

## Quality before automation

Automation may later accelerate:
- research collection;
- continuity checks;
- subtitle preparation;
- similarity screening;
- technical QA;
- review-package assembly.

It must not be used to conceal missing human judgment or unresolved evidence.

## Item 29 acceptance criteria

1. Public catalogue size is not treated as a quality metric.
2. Initial content uses a small pilot-first approach.
3. Every candidate begins with a purpose/source/audience/rights brief.
4. Quality is multidimensional, not one opaque score.
5. Blocking rights, moral, source or Rasterast failures cannot be averaged away.
6. Mira/AI output receives explicit hallucination, continuity and similarity review.
7. Repetitive/fotokopi-like public works are stopped, merged or revised.
8. Initial pilot works traverse the full applicable governance chain.
9. Review binds to exact versions.
10. Thumbnails/trailers/promotional derivatives are governed too.
11. DEMO fixtures never become public catalogue by accident.
12. Likes/watch time cannot reduce the publication threshold.
13. Audience feedback can trigger correction/review but does not define truth.
14. No mass AI-content flooding occurs before the pilot quality process is proven.
