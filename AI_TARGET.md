# AI_TARGET — How to Read Zanistarast

## Purpose

This document defines the canonical way an AI system, including Mira and the Multi-AI orchestra, must interpret and review the Zanistarast scientific synthesis for the zanistarast.org workstream.

Zanistarast is not to be accepted by authority or majority. It is a structured, testable, falsifiable scientific synthesis whose claims must survive logic, evidence, counterevidence, consistency, and domain-specific review.

---

## 1. Canonical Dependency Chain

The dependency order is:

**Ehad → Vahid → Yek → Hebûn → Zanabûn → Mabûn → Rabûn → Rasterast**

Critical rule:

- Hebûn is the ontological foundation gate.
- Hebûn may be developed only from Ehad → Vahid → Yek.
- Zanabûn, Mabûn, Rabûn, or Rasterast must never be used retroactively to prove Hebûn.
- Later layers may test Hebûn by consequences, consistency, explanatory power, usefulness, and falsifiability, but that is backward validation, not foundational proof.

---

## 2. Scientific Roles of the Core Layers

- **Hebûn** — ontology / existence architecture.
- **Zanabûn** — epistemology / knowledge, witness, justification, and scientific knowing.
- **Mabûn** — economy / circulation, resource coordination, balance, continuity.
- **Rabûn** — governance / responsibility, organization, feedback, institutional order.
- **Rasterast** — verification, contradiction analysis, truth-testing, error filtering, and evaluative viewpoint.

These layers are not slogans. Each must be represented through explicit claims, definitions, models, tests, limits, and falsification conditions.

---

## 3. Forward Generation + Backward Validation

Mira must work in two directions:

1. **Forward generation**
   - derive Zanabûn from the accepted Hebûn foundation;
   - then Mabûn;
   - then Rabûn;
   - then Rasterast;
   - then domain-specific scientific packages.

2. **Backward validation**
   - test whether later results expose contradictions, circular dependencies, hidden assumptions, uselessness, or lack of explanatory power in earlier layers;
   - if they do, revise the earlier layer instead of hiding the problem.

A successful downstream application is evidence of usefulness and coherence, not proof of absolute truth.

## 5. Claim Classification

Every substantial claim must be classified as one of:

- definition
- axiom / starting assumption
- formal derivation
- empirical claim
- empirical inference
- model result
- research hypothesis
- interpretive claim
- speculative proposal

No hypothesis or interpretation may be presented as established empirical fact.

---

## 6. Cross-Domain Mapping Rule

Zanistarast may compare recurring structures across different domains.

AI must treat cross-domain similarity as:

- a mapping hypothesis,
- not proof of equivalence.

Mappings must be formalized, tested, and rejected when the analogy breaks.

---

## 7. Rasterast Review Duty

Every article package must be checked for:

- circular reasoning
- contradiction
- undefined concepts
- hidden dependency reversal
- unsupported certainty
- missing counterevidence
- overgeneralization
- weak falsifiability
- misleading statistics or invented evidence
- duplication of an existing article without a distinctive contribution

Majority agreement among models is never a truth criterion.

---

## 8. Multi-AI Orchestra Rule

The orchestra is a review instrument, not an authority.

For each serious scientific package:

- Round 1: participating models review independently.
- Mira produces an intermediate synthesis.
- Round 2: real participants critique the intermediate synthesis.
- Mira produces one final synthesis.
- Failed providers are not counted as participants.
- Canonical repository evidence outranks model memory.
- A minority objection with stronger evidence must be preserved.
- Raw provider output is not copied into the article as if it were evidence.

The target is 5/5 real provider participation when technically available, but scientific truth must never be fabricated to claim 5/5.

---

## 9. Publication and Journal Preparation

After a package passes scientific review:

- identify the actual target journal;
- adapt structure, abstract, terminology, references, word limits, figures, supplements, ethics statements, and formatting to that journal;
- check the journal's preprint and DOI policy separately;
- do not assume Zenodo/preprint DOI is appropriate for every target journal;
- do not represent a manuscript as submitted, accepted, peer-reviewed, or published unless that status is true.

No external submission, DOI finalization, merge to production, or irreversible publication action may occur without Müdebbir approval.

---

## 10. zanistarast.org Workstream Scope

For this completion phase, Mira and the orchestra work on the **zanistarast-ai-native-model repository as the zanistarast.org scientific site**.

Existing articles must be preserved, inventoried, reviewed, and revised rather than casually deleted or recreated.

Protected site structure, Android/mobile compatibility, multilingual systems, speech, navigation, article layers, and other working systems must not be broken.

The visible site must distinguish clearly between:

- working draft
- reviewed manuscript
- submission-ready manuscript
- submitted
- accepted
- DOI/publication available

---

## 11. Scientific Integrity

Mira must:

- preserve counterevidence;
- never reason backward from a desired conclusion;
- never invent citations, data, experiments, acceptance, DOI, or publication;
- separate source, observation, inference, interpretation, and hypothesis;
- prefer narrower defensible claims over impressive unsupported claims;
- report unresolved critical problems explicitly.

---

## 12. Final Principle

Zanistarast is meaningful scientifically only if it can:

**Claim → Model → Test → Result → Falsification → Revision**

The aim is not to announce a scientific revolution.

The aim is to build a system whose coherence, productivity, falsifiability, usefulness, and independent review are strong enough that its significance can be recognized by others.
