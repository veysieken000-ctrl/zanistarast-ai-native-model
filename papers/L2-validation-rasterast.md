# L2 – Validation: Consistency, Verification, and System Integrity (Rasterast)

## Abstract

This paper explores the role of validation mechanisms in maintaining system stability across natural, computational, and human domains.

Zanistarast defines this layer as **L2 – Validation (Rasterast)**,
a structural process that ensures coherence, correctness, and continuity.

---

## 1. Introduction

Engineered systems often require explicit consistency constraints, while natural and social systems need domain-specific descriptions and tests.

If inputs contradict structure,
if actions violate rules,
or if states become incoherent,

→ the system destabilizes.

Zanistarast models this requirement as:

> L2 – Validation (Rasterast)

---

## 2. Mathematical Perspective

In mathematics:

- equations must satisfy constraints
- proofs must follow logical consistency
- contradictions invalidate systems

A contradiction invalidates a classical proof under its assumptions; software may instead reject, isolate, log or recover from an inconsistent input.

This implies:

> Validation is not optional—it is foundational.

---

## 3. Computational Systems

Modern computing systems rely heavily on validation:

- authentication (e.g., multi-factor verification)
- syntax checking
- logical constraints
- runtime error handling

Examples:

- invalid input → system rejection
- failed authentication → access denied
- inconsistent state → crash or rollback

This shows:

> Some engineered systems use validation to reduce particular errors, subject to false positives, false negatives and overhead.

---

## 4. Natural Systems

In nature:

- physical laws enforce consistency
- incompatible states cannot persist
- unstable configurations collapse

Examples:

- unstable atomic structures decay
- ecosystems collapse when constraints are violated
- biological systems fail when internal balance is broken

This suggests:

> Physical laws describe dynamics; they do not establish that nature contains a universal fact-checking mechanism.

---

## 5. Human Systems

In social and organizational systems:

- rules define acceptable behavior
- governance enforces consistency
- violations produce instability

Examples:

- economic imbalance → crisis
- legal inconsistency → conflict
- institutional corruption → collapse

Thus:

> Validation is required for social stability.

## 12. Testable verification protocol (scientific revision, 2026-10-09)

**Rasterast:** Keep syntax, logical consistency, source entailment, empirical accuracy, confidence calibration and policy compliance as separate tests. For factual claims, record source passage, date, independent reference label and abstention. Include coherent-but-false negative controls and report false-positive/false-negative rates and verification latency. Compare verification-on/off only under matched offered workload; account for verification overhead. **Alternative explanation:** verification may be activated preferentially during high load, confounding a simple observational stability comparison. A checksum establishes byte integrity, not truth. The proposed protocol has not yet been executed or independently reviewed.
